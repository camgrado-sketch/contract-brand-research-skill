# Anonymized Example: Premium Public-Space Brand

This example demonstrates the workflow without identifying a real brand or including proprietary research.

## User request

> “This brand appears to offer a focused, premium product range. Why can it still sustain meaningful revenue in large public-space projects?”

## Phase 1: Alignment Brief

- **Core question:** Explain how a focused premium offer can produce scale in public-space projects.
- **Working assumption to test:** High prices necessarily mean low volume and limited project access.
- **Reference frame:** Commercial model and organizational public-space use.
- **Decision use:** Inform a product-platform and channel-priority decision.
- **Scope:** Global brand; current public evidence; concise research note.
- **Priority dimensions:** D2 Product system, D4 Channel, D5 Projects, D6 Defensibility.

## Phase 2: What the agent should test

1. Is the offer actually low-volume, or is it a standardized platform with many repeatable configurations?
2. What project functions create quantity demand: stacking, linking, mobility, storage, durability, or service?
3. Who specifies the product, who transacts, and who installs or maintains it?
4. Does evidence show a lifecycle, compliance, or operational value beyond purchase price?
5. What data could overturn the user's assumption?

## Example evidence boundary

> **Fact:** An official report states a product-life or spare-parts policy.
>
> **Analysis:** This policy can reduce operational replacement risk in a high-frequency public space.
>
> **Unverified:** The policy directly causes a particular gross margin or win rate. Public evidence may not support this claim.

## Phase 3: Optional knowledge sync

If the user requests knowledge sync, map the research note to the target vault's positioning, expression, and evidence templates. Review empty template fields, top up only official-source gaps, show paths for approval, and create a pull request rather than directly changing the release branch.
