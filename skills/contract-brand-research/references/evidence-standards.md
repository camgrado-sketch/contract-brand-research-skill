# Evidence Standards

## Statement classes

Every material conclusion must be classified before publication.

| Class | Definition | Required treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Fact** | Directly stated in a source of known scope | Cite source, date, scope, and page or section when available. |
| **Analysis** | A reasoned interpretation based on named facts | State the inference and list the supporting facts. |
| **Unverified** | Plausible claim without adequate support | State that evidence is missing; do not use as a conclusion. |

A brand's own positioning language can be a Fact **about what the brand says**. It is not automatically a Fact about customer perception, commercial performance, or product superiority.

## Source grades

| Grade | Source type | Typical use | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Statutory filings, annual reports, audited or sustainability reports, certification registries | Scale, operations, policies, verified claims | Check entity and reporting-year scope. |
| B | Official corporate website, official catalogues, regional official sites, official product data | Brand self-description, product, project, channel claims | May be marketing-led or incomplete. |
| C | Established trade press, professional design media, credible analyst coverage | Cross-checks and independent context | Confirm date, attribution, and possible sponsorship. |
| D | Dealer pages, trade-show materials, third-party business databases | Lead generation and bounded supplementary context | May be stale, selective, or derivative. |
| E | Social posts, forums, anonymous blogs, unsourced aggregators | Search leads only | Do not cite as decision evidence. |

## Data integrity checks

Before using any number, answer all five questions:

| Check | Question |
|---|---|
| Entity | Does the figure describe the group, a subsidiary, a country business, or a product line? |
| Time | What year, period, and reporting status does it describe? |
| Denominator | What is the base of this percentage or ratio? |
| Unit | Is it currency, units, seats, projects, employees, or another measure? |
| Boundary | What cannot legitimately be inferred from it? |

Examples of invalid inference:

- Supplier-country share → sales-country share.
- Export percentage → number of international customers.
- One project quantity → typical annual order size.
- Revenue divided by all products shipped → actual average selling price.
- Official product count → active sell-through or revenue contribution.

## Confidence language

| Confidence | Use when | Suggested wording |
|---|---|---|
| High | Multiple A/B sources agree and scope is clear | “Official reporting indicates…” |
| Medium | One strong source or several consistent C sources | “Available evidence suggests…” |
| Low | Partial evidence, unclear scope, or indirect indicators | “This is a tentative interpretation…” |
| Not assessable | Evidence is unavailable | “Public evidence was not found; this remains unverified.” |

## Illustrative calculations

Calculations must show:

1. Formula.
2. Source and period for every input.
3. Any mismatch in time or scope.
4. Explicit label: `illustrative calculation`, not observed commercial fact.

Do not calculate personal, sensitive, or undisclosed financial data from speculation.
