# Phase 2: Evidence-Backed Research and Analysis

## Purpose

Answer the confirmed Alignment Brief with evidence that can change, confirm, or reject the user's working assumption. Research must explain a mechanism, not merely summarize a brand website.

## Research order

Use the following sequence. Do not begin with images, generic news, or competitor lists.

| Order | Evidence goal | Preferred source | Typical result |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Scale and operating anchors | Annual reports, sustainability reports, statutory filings | Revenue, employees, production, exports, sites, time period |
| 2 | Brand self-definition | About page, manifesto, official catalogue opening and closing pages | Brand language, claimed purpose, positioning |
| 3 | Product platform | Official product pages, usage areas, BIM/CAD libraries, catalogues | Series, configuration logic, CMF, technical functions |
| 4 | Project and customer evidence | Official references, architect portfolios, case studies | Space types, client types, deployment patterns |
| 5 | Channel and service evidence | Service pages, dealer locator, regional subsidiaries, showrooms | Sales roles, specification paths, delivery model |
| 6 | External cross-check | Specialist trade media, awards, public registries | Independent confirmation or discrepancy |

Use only the rows relevant to the confirmed dimensions. Do not research all six dimensions by default.

## Source ledger

Create a ledger before drawing strong conclusions. Use `templates/evidence-list.md`.

For every decision-relevant claim, record:

- Claim.
- Source URL or file identity.
- Source grade.
- Date and reporting period.
- Entity scope: group, subsidiary, market, product line, or project.
- Classification: Fact, Analysis, or Unverified.
- Boundary: what the evidence cannot prove.

## Analysis procedure

1. **Build anchors.** Find several hard numbers or directly observable operating facts.
2. **Test the assumption.** Look for information that could falsify the user's intuition, not only information that supports it.
3. **Find the mechanism.** Explain how product platform, design, production, channel, project configuration, and service interact.
4. **Use deepening prompts only where needed.**
5. **Separate the external brand from the user's self-brand mapping.** The latter is a strategy judgment, not market fact.

## Deepening prompts

| Prompt | Use when | Example form |
|---|---|---|
| Contradiction | Two facts do not appear to fit | If A is true, why does B also occur? |
| Substitute | Pricing or selection needs explanation | Why does the buyer not choose a lower-cost alternative? |
| Assumption | A common shorthand drives the conclusion | What must be true for this conclusion to hold? |
| Scale | A claim feels implausible or abstract | What does revenue divided by output, employees, or projects imply? |
| Boundary | Transferability is at issue | Under what market, channel, or procurement condition would this logic fail? |

## Quantitative discipline

- Use a calculation tool for arithmetic. Do not estimate mentally.
- Label ratios made from different periods or scopes as **illustrative**, not observed averages.
- Never infer sales geography from procurement geography, nor group performance from one legal entity without explicit evidence.
- Treat a single flagship project as a case study, not a representative sample.
- Do not infer margin, rebate, bid success rate, or customer concentration without direct evidence.

## Required analysis structure

Unless the Alignment Brief requests another form, organize the result as:

1. **Answer first** — a concise answer to the core question.
2. **What the evidence changes** — working assumption versus supported view.
3. **Mechanism** — 3–5 causal components, with facts and analysis clearly separated.
4. **Counterpoint and boundary** — where the mechanism is not proven or may not transfer.
5. **Self-brand mapping** — only when requested or configured.
6. **Evidence note** — source quality, time anchors, and missing data.

## Output labels

Use these labels visibly in research notes:

> **Fact** — directly stated by a credible source.
>
> **Analysis** — a reasoned interpretation grounded in named facts.
>
> **Unverified** — plausible but not sufficiently supported; do not present as a conclusion.

## Stop conditions

End evidence collection when:

- The core question can be answered with multiple independent anchors or a stated insufficiency.
- Additional browsing is repeating the same claim rather than testing the mechanism.
- Each major conclusion has a source label and boundary.
- The user-requested depth and output have been achieved.
